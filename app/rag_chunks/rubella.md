# Rubella

**Aliases:** German measles

## Description
contagious viral infection characterized by mild fever and rash, but can cause severe birth defects if contracted during pregnancy

## Cause
rubella virus (a togavirus)

## Transmission
airborne droplets from coughing or sneezing, direct contact, mother-to-fetus during pregnancy

## Risk Groups
unvaccinated individuals, pregnant women, travelers to endemic regions, children

## Incubation Period
14-21 days

## Symptoms
- mild fever
- rash starting on face and spreading to body
- sore throat
- swollen lymph nodes
- joint pain (especially in adult women)
- fatigue

## Progression
- exposure to virus via respiratory droplets
- viral replication in respiratory tract
- spread through bloodstream
- development of rash and mild systemic symptoms

## Common Locations
skin, lymph nodes, respiratory tract, fetus (in congenital infection)

## Duration
1-2 weeks

## Severity
mild_to_moderate

## Complications
- arthritis, encephalitis (rare), congenital rubella syndrome, miscarriage, birth defects

## Home Remedies
- rest, hydration, fever management with acetaminophen

## Things to Avoid
- contact with pregnant women, skipping vaccination, crowded environments during infection

## When to See a Doctor
rash with fever, exposure history, pregnancy exposure

## Emergency Signs
severe headache, confusion, neurological symptoms

## Prevention
- MMR vaccination, isolation during infection, hygiene practices

## Contagious Period
about 1 week before rash to 7 days after rash onset

## Special Notes
often mild in children but dangerous in pregnancy, lifelong immunity after infection or vaccination, no specific antiviral treatment
